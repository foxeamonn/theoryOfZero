Introducing Point zero.

In any system of forces, there will be one point where the force acting will be zero. The point most people will be familiar with is the fulcrum of a lever. For an body or object, this will be it's centre of mass, and for two bodies not in contact with each other, this will be their barycentre. I will use the terms interchangeably throughout this essay.

The law of the lever.

"Give me a lever long enough and a place to stand, and I shall move the world". Thus spake Archimedes. What he was talking about of course, was the relationship between force and distance. The greater the distance of the applied force from the fulcrum, the more magnified the input force becomes, and the less the output force is. This implies there must be a universal constant linking force and distance. For the sake of tradition I call call this constant G. Regardless of the force applied, the gradient (output force / input force) will follow an inverse square law. Relative motion.

If all motion is relative, what is a body moving relative to? The answer is that a body is moving relative to all other bodies it does not share a common centre of mass with. As shown earlier, the ratio of applied force to input force depends on distance. As motion is a change in distance, then motion can be described as the change in ratio of applied to input forces. Although the ratio changes, the nett force remains zero at the fulcrum (barycentre) if the bodies are not in contact with each other. To an observer however, it is always the other body who is moving and they will consider themselves to be at rest. In actual reality, both observers are moving as it is not possible to have only one body moving in a system.
